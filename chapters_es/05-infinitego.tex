Lets characterize \textit{what} can be done in the game.

\begin{definition}[A move]
	In an infinite game of Go of dimension $n$, a player in their turn
	$t$ can perform one of the following actions:
	\begin{enumerate}
		1. Pass.
		
		2. Propose a connected hyperplane $H$ of dimension at most
		$n-1$. The hyperplane $H$ is a subset of the board such that,
		for at least one dimension, all its points have a fixed
		coordinate. All the stones in $H$ have the color of the player
		making the move. In particular, if $\dim H=0$, then $H$ is asingle stone.
		
		The stones already present on the board may intersect $H$. Let
		\[
		P=\{P(x)\in T_t:C(x)\neq 0\}
		\]
		be the set of occupied points at turn $t$. The stones that are
		actually played are then
		\[
		H - P=\{h\in H: P(h)\notin P\}.
		\]
		The set $H\setminus P$ may consist of several groups, as the
		stones already present on the board may disconnect the proposed
		hyperplane.
		
		If any group contained in $H-P$ is in suicide
		condition, the proposed move is rejected. Otherwise, the
		stones in $H-P$ are placed on the board, and captures
		are resolved according to the usual rules.
	\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
Let me explain why $n-1$ dimensions.

If we look back at the game of Go, stones are placed on a two dimensional board. In our formulation, this means that we place a connected $0$ dimensional subset of the board, that is, a single stone. In other words, the dimension of the object being played is $n-2$ relative to the dimension of the board.

If we kept this relation, we would obtain a natural generalization for higher dimensional boards, but we wouldn't be unable to define a move in one dimensional Go, since this would require
$$ n-2=-1. $$
Thus, in an $n$ dimensional game, a player may propose a connected hyperplane $H$ satisfying
$$ 0\leq\dim H\leq n-1. $$

We cannot use Zobrist method for the infinite Go as we will need infinite many bits. We need a better method, let's try to make one.

If we remember the nature of the SuperKo rule, it's says that we cannot replicate a prior board via a stone play. Again, we are using this version of SuperKo (Positional).

Because of the rule, every game has to be a line, that is, as we cannot go back into the history of plays (at least it's a pass but will not take in consideration those). From the empty board to the very end, we have a straight forward path. Not even that but, because of the rule, the whole game of Go its a \textit{tree}. That is, starting from the empty board, there are 362 possbile plays (361 stone placements and a pass) and we cannot go back. From those, every posible outcome, that is, any end of a match can be found by following a line of unrepeated boards.

\begin{definition}[Dense hyperplane]
	Given a proposed hyperplane $H$, we say that $H$ is dense
	if every stone $t\in H$ is occupied, that is,
	$$C(t)\neq 0 \quad \forall t\in H.$$
\end{definition}
